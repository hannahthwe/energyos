## 3-minute pitch script

Audience assumption: energy and AI specialists rather than security specialists. Core message: **nobody will let an AI move energy in millions of homes unless its decisions can be checked — security unlocks flexibility.**

| Time | Slide | Script |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | **Hook** | “It’s 2:14 at night. Your electric car starts powering your house. You didn’t ask it to. Was it the charger? The solar inverter? The AI that manages your home’s energy? Or an attacker? Today, nobody can tell you — and that is the problem we solve. I’m [name], a PhD researcher in AI and cybersecurity for energy systems, and this is EnergyOS Secure.” |
| 0:20–0:50 | **Problem** | “A modern Dutch home is a small power plant: solar panels, a battery, a heat pump, an EV — each from a different brand — and increasingly an AI decides when energy flows. When something goes wrong, the charger maker sees a command, the energy platform sees a strange login, the inverter maker sees odd power flow, and the AI simply acted on a price it was given. Everyone holds one clue; nobody sees the whole picture. And this is not theory: this autumn hackers switched supermarket fridges across Europe, including here in the Netherlands, into defrost mode. Homes run the same kind of connected controllers.” |
| 0:50–1:10 | **Why now** | “Why now, and why the Netherlands? Almost 790,000 electric cars, more than 3.2 million solar installations, and net metering ends in January, so homes will store and shift energy with software. The EU’s Cyber Resilience Act gives manufacturers 24 hours to warn about an exploited flaw — which means knowing within hours which homes are affected. And AI is speeding attackers up.” |
| 1:10–1:50 | **Product** | “EnergyOS Secure is the security layer for AI-run homes. It runs beside the home’s energy system — it doesn’t replace it. First, it checks every command the AI sends: does discharging the car at 2 a.m. make sense, given the price, the battery and the owner’s departure time? Second, it joins the clues from every brand into one case and tells you whether it looks like an attack, a fault or a mistake. Third, it finds every other home running the same vulnerable software. Fourth, it tells each company exactly what it must do — while people stay in charge. Back to our car at 2:14: the command is flagged within seconds, with the full story attached.” |
| 1:50–2:20 | **Why us** | “Why hasn’t anyone done this? Brands secure only their own devices, security tools don’t understand energy physics, and companies refuse to pool their data. That last barrier is exactly what my PhD solves: hierarchical federated learning detects cyberattacks early in power grids without centralising anyone’s data. I simulate attacks and build the detection and correlation algorithms this product needs, and through an EU project with 75 organisations in 15 countries I already know the people who will buy and test it.” |
| 2:20–2:40 | **Business** | “We sell to the companies that run home energy — energy platforms and charger makers — as a yearly licence plus a small fee per home, so revenue grows as their fleets grow. We start in the Netherlands, then Belgium and Germany. Three platforms and two manufacturers would already be around 320,000 euros a year. The big unknown is willingness to pay, so our first milestone is a paid pilot that measures it.” |
| 2:40–3:00 | **Ask** | “Nobody will let an AI move energy in millions of homes unless its decisions can be checked. We make that check possible. We are asking for two design partners, one paid pilot, and 1.2 million euros pre-seed to prove it over 24 months. Security is what unlocks flexibility. Thank you.” |

**If time runs short:** drop the third point on the “Why us” slide and the AI sentence on “Why now”.

All business figures are illustrative planning assumptions; facts are referenced in the business plan (section 18). Verify news items against primary sources before presenting.
